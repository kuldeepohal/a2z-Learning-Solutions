Chapter 1: The Wonderful World of Science

Important Questions • Class 6 Science

1. What is science? Why is curiosity important in science?

Science is presented as a way of exploring and understanding the world. Curiosity encourages us to notice things, ask questions and investigate them.

2. Explain why science is compared to an unending jigsaw puzzle.

The comparison highlights that science is an ongoing journey. Each discovery can help explain something while also leading to new questions.

3. Describe the scientific method introduced in the chapter.

The chapter introduces a sequence involving observation, asking a question, proposing a possible answer, testing it through experiments or observations, and analysing the results.

4. How can an everyday observation become a scientific investigation?

An observation can make us curious. We can turn that curiosity into a question, propose a possible explanation, test it and analyse what we observe.

5. Give examples of questions that can arise from everyday observations.

Examples include asking why a paper boat floats while a stone sinks, why a ball rolls differently on different surfaces, or why water droplets form on the outside of a cold glass.

6. What is the purpose of Activity 1.1?

Activity 1.1 encourages students to make a list of questions about a familiar object such as a chair, pen or book, showing that science can begin with ordinary things.

7. Why is testing important after proposing a possible answer?

Testing provides observations or experimental results that can be analysed to see what the evidence shows about the possible answer.

8. How does science help us understand the world around us?

Science encourages systematic observation, questioning, investigation and evidence-based understanding of the natural world and everyday phenomena.
