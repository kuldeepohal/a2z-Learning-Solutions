🔑 Key Terms & Keywords

ScienceA way of thinking, observing and doing things to understand the world.
CuriosityThe desire to wonder, ask questions and explore.
ObservationCarefully noticing something around us.
QuestionSomething we ask when we want to understand or explain an observation.
Possible answerAn idea or explanation that can be checked.
ExperimentA way of testing an idea by doing something and observing the result.
AnalyseExamining results to understand what they tell us.
Scientific methodA step-by-step approach of observing, questioning, suggesting, testing and analysing.
DiscoveryNew knowledge found through exploration and investigation.
TeamworkWorking with others to investigate questions and solve problems.

Key Points to Remember

01 Science helps us understand the world and uncover the secrets of the universe.

02 Curiosity is an important starting point for scientific learning.

03 Science is an unending jigsaw puzzle; discoveries can lead to more questions.

04 Earth supports life and its environment needs protection.

05 Everyday things—food, materials, water, temperature and objects—can be explored scientifically.

06 A scientific approach follows a step-by-step process for finding answers.

07 Testing a possible answer through experiments or observations is an important part of the method.

08 Science is often collaborative; people can discover and solve problems together.

The Scientific Method

1 • ObserveNotice something interesting or something you do not understand.
2 • QuestionWonder about it and form a question.
3 • Possible answerSuggest a possible explanation.
4 • TestUse experiments or further observations.
5 • AnalyseExamine the results to see whether they answer the question.
Pen example: If a pen stops writing, you might suspect that the ink is finished. You check the refill. If it is not empty, make another guess and test again.

Daily-Life Examples

  • Finding out why dal has spilled from a cooker.
  • Finding where air is leaking from a flat bicycle tyre.
  • Finding whether a bulb or switch is responsible when a light does not work.
  • Investigating why a pen has stopped writing.

Chapter Activities

  1. Activity 1.1: Write about a problem you tried to solve and the steps you took.
  2. Activity 1.2: Describe a daily-life situation where someone was following the scientific method.
  3. Activity 1.3: Choose something you want to ask “Why?” about, then write how you would try to find the answer.
