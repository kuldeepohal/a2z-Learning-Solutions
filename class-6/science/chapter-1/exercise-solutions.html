1. Why is curiosity important in Science?

Answer: Curiosity makes us wonder about things around us and encourages us to ask how and why. It is the starting point for exploring, observing and finding scientific answers.

2. Explain Science as an unending jigsaw puzzle.

Answer: Science is compared with a giant unending jigsaw puzzle because every discovery adds a new piece of knowledge. A new discovery can also lead to more questions or change our understanding of an earlier idea.

3. Give examples of Science in everyday life.

Answer: Science can be seen in many everyday situations, such as understanding food, materials, water, temperature and objects around us. Solving why a pen stops writing, finding an air leak in a bicycle tyre, or checking why a bulb does not work are examples of scientific thinking.

5. Can ordinary people work like scientists?

Answer: Yes. The chapter explains that anyone who follows a scientific approach to solve a problem is working like a scientist. A cook, bicycle repair person or electrician may observe a problem, ask questions, test possible explanations and examine results.

6. Why should we observe our surroundings keenly?

Answer: Keen observation helps us notice interesting things and raises questions. These questions lead to investigation and learning. The chapter encourages students to keep observing, wondering and exploring.

7. Why is teamwork useful in Science?

Answer: Scientific work is often done by teams. When we cannot find an answer alone, friends or other people can help. Working together can make discovering and solving problems more effective and enjoyable.
