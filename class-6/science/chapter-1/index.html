📚 Chapter Notes — Important Concepts

1. What is Science?

Science is a way of thinking, observing and doing things to understand the world around us and uncover the secrets of the universe.

The chapter presents science as an adventure: we ask questions, explore the world and try to understand how things work. The most important starting point is curiosity.

2. Science is Everywhere

Science can be found in everyday life and in the natural world—from grains of sand and mountains to kitchens, playgrounds, oceans, forests and outer space.

New discoveries add pieces to an unending jigsaw puzzle of knowledge. New evidence can change our understanding and lead to more questions.

3. What We Explore

  • Earth: the only planet currently known to us that supports life.
  • Living world: plants and animals survive and thrive in different regions.
  • Food: ingredients and different cuisines.
  • Materials: paper, metal, plastic, rubber, magnets, clothes and other objects around us.
  • Water: rain, freezing, boiling, ice, steam and understanding hot and cold.
  • Space: the Sun, Moon and stars.

4. Science in Daily Life

The chapter explains that ordinary problem-solving can follow scientific thinking. Examples include finding why a pen stops writing, why dal spills from a cooker, where air leaks from a bicycle tyre, or why a bulb does not work.

Key idea: Anyone who follows the scientific method is, in a way, working like a scientist.

🔑 Important Terms & Concepts

  • Curiosity: the desire to know, explore and ask questions.
  • Observation: noticing something interesting or something we do not understand.
  • Question: what we ask when an observation makes us wonder.
  • Guess / Possible answer: a proposed answer that can be tested.
  • Experiment: a way to test a possible answer.
  • Analysis: examining results to see whether they answer the question.
  • Scientific method: a step-by-step process for finding answers to questions.
  • Scientific inquiry: asking questions, testing ideas and learning from observations/results.

📝 Quick Summary

  1. Science is a way of thinking, observing and doing things to understand the world.
  2. Curiosity is the starting point of scientific learning.
  3. Science is present in everyday life as well as in Earth, nature and outer space.
  4. Scientific knowledge keeps developing; new discoveries can change earlier understanding.
  5. Science uses a step-by-step process: observe, ask a question, propose a possible answer, test it, and analyse the results.
  6. Everyday problem-solving can use the scientific method.
  7. Learning science develops our ability to find solutions to bigger problems and explore mysteries.
  8. Scientists often work together, so collaboration can make discovery more effective and enjoyable.
  9. The chapter encourages students to remain curious, observe keenly and keep asking questions.
One-line revision: Curiosity → Observation → Question → Possible answer → Test → Analyse → Learn / explore further.

✅ Exercise / Activity Solutions

The uploaded chapter contains three “Let us think and write” activities rather than a conventional end-of-chapter exercise. The answers below are model answers; students can write their own examples too. fileciteturn0file0L146-L152

Activity 1.1 — Write about a similar problem that you tried to solve. What steps did you take?

Model answer: My pen stopped writing. First, I observed the problem and asked why it had stopped. I guessed that the ink might have finished. I opened the pen and checked the refill. When the refill was not empty, I considered another possible reason: the ink might have dried. I then tried another way to test the new guess. This follows the scientific method because I observed, questioned, proposed possible answers, tested them and examined the results. fileciteturn0file0L126-L145

Activity 1.2 — Describe a daily-life situation where someone was following a scientific method.

Model answer: A bicycle repair person finds that a tyre is flat. They ask where the air is leaking, inspect the tyre and test possible locations for the leak. The observations and tests help identify the cause. This is an example of applying scientific thinking in daily life. fileciteturn0file0L189-L201

Activity 1.3 — If you have to ask “Why?” about something, what would you ask? How would you attempt to find an answer?

Model answer: Question: “Why does water become ice when it is cooled?” I would observe what happens while cooling water, ask what change occurs, make a possible explanation, test it through repeated observation/experiment and analyse the results. The chapter itself uses the change of water into ice on cooling and steam on heating as an example of questions that science can help us explore. fileciteturn0file0L100-L109
