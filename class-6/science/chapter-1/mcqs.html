Chapter 1: The Wonderful World of Science — MCQs

Class 6 Science • Chapter-wise revision practice

1. What is science described as in the chapter?

  1. A collection of fixed facts
  2. A way of exploring and understanding the world
  3. Only the study of machines
  4. Only laboratory work
Answer: B. Science involves exploring and understanding the world around us.

2. What can trigger scientific investigation?

  1. Curiosity about something observed
  2. Memorising a definition
  3. Copying an answer
  4. Avoiding questions
Answer: A. Curiosity and observations can lead us to ask questions and investigate.

3. Which sequence best represents the scientific method introduced in the chapter?

  1. Guess → memorise → stop
  2. Observe → question → possible answer → test → analyse
  3. Read → copy → submit
  4. Question → ignore → conclude
Answer: B. The chapter presents observation, questioning, a possible answer, testing and analysis as key steps.

4. Why can a scientific question be called the beginning of a journey?

  1. It closes the investigation immediately
  2. It leads to investigation and further understanding
  3. It replaces observation
  4. It removes the need for evidence
Answer: B. A question can lead us to investigate and discover more.

5. Which activity in the chapter asks students to make a list of questions about a common object?

  1. Activity 1.1
  2. Activity 1.2
  3. Activity 1.3
  4. Activity 2.1
Answer: A. Activity 1.1 encourages students to think of questions about an object such as a chair, pen or book.

6. Which example illustrates investigation through observation and questioning?

  1. Wondering why a paper boat floats while a stone sinks
  2. Reciting a poem
  3. Writing a name repeatedly
  4. Copying a timetable
Answer: A. The chapter uses everyday observations such as floating and sinking to encourage scientific questioning.

7. What is one reason the chapter compares science to an unending jigsaw puzzle?

  1. All answers are already known
  2. There are always new questions and discoveries
  3. Science uses only puzzles
  4. Experiments are unnecessary
Answer: B. Science continues to grow as new observations raise new questions.

8. What should happen after testing a possible answer?

  1. The results should be analysed
  2. The results should be hidden
  3. The question should be forgotten
  4. The experiment should be ignored
Answer: A. Analysing results helps determine what the investigation shows.
