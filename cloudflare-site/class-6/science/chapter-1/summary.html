Chapter Summary

The Wonderful World of Science — the chapter introduces Science as a curious, question-driven journey of observing, thinking, testing and discovering.

Read Full ExplanationJump to Key Points

At a Glance

ThemeScience as a way of thinking, observing and doing things to understand the world.
Starting pointCuriosity — wondering about what we observe and asking questions.
Science around usScience is everywhere: nature, Earth, food, materials, water, daily life and outer space.
Big ideaScience is like a giant, unending jigsaw puzzle. Discoveries add pieces and can change our understanding.
MethodObserve → question → guess a possible answer → test → analyse results.
PeopleScientists use the scientific method, but anyone using it to solve a problem is working like a scientist.
Learning attitudeBe curious, observe keenly, ask “how?” and “why?”, work with others and keep exploring.

Key Points to Remember

01 Science helps us understand the world and uncover the secrets of the universe.

02 Curiosity is the most important starting point for scientific learning.

03 Science has no final limit because every new discovery can lead to more questions.

04 Earth supports life and its environment needs protection.

05 Everyday things—food, materials, water, temperature and objects—can be explored scientifically.

06 A scientific approach does not simply memorise facts; it follows a step-by-step process for finding answers.

07 Testing a possible answer through experiments or observations is an important part of the method.

08 Science is often collaborative; people can discover and solve problems together.

The Scientific Method

1 • ObserveNotice something interesting or something you do not understand.
2 • QuestionWonder about it and form a question.
3 • Guess & TestSuggest a possible answer and test it with experiments or further observations.
4 • AnalyseExamine the results to see whether they answer the question.
Pen example: If a pen stops writing, you might first suspect that the ink is finished. You check the refill. If it is not empty, you make another guess and test again.

Daily-Life Examples

  • Finding out why dal has spilled from a cooker.
  • Finding where air is leaking from a flat bicycle tyre.
  • Finding whether a bulb or switch is responsible when a light does not work.
  • Investigating why a pen has stopped writing.

These examples show that scientific thinking can happen in ordinary situations.

Chapter Activities

  1. Activity 1.1: Write about a problem you tried to solve and the steps you took.
  2. Activity 1.2: Describe a daily-life situation where someone was following the scientific method.
  3. Activity 1.3: Choose something you want to ask “Why?” about, then write how you would try to find the answer.

Quick Revision

One-line revision: Science starts with curiosity, grows through questions and observations, and finds answers by testing ideas and analysing results.

Memory chain: Curiosity → Observe → Question → Possible answer → Test → Analyse → Discover

The chapter closes with an invitation to enjoy the journey of Science, keep exploring, never stop wondering and keep asking questions.
