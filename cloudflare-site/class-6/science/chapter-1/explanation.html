Grade 6 • Science • Chapter 1

The Wonderful World of Science

Science begins with curiosity. This chapter introduces Science as a way of thinking, observing, asking questions, testing ideas and learning from evidence.

Quick SummaryLearn the Scientific Method

1. What is Science?

The chapter presents Science as a way of thinking, observing and doing things to understand the world around us and uncover the secrets of the universe.

Science is described as a big adventure: we ask questions, explore the world and try to understand how things work. The most important starting point is curiosity.

Key idea: Science is not limited to a laboratory. It is everywhere—from tiny grains of sand and a leaf of grass to mountains, oceans, the kitchen, the playground and outer space.

2. Science is an Unending Jigsaw Puzzle

The chapter compares Science with a giant, unending jigsaw puzzle. Each new discovery adds another piece. Because every new piece of knowledge can lead to more questions, there is no final limit to what we can discover.

Sometimes a discovery shows that an earlier idea was in the wrong place. New discoveries can therefore change our understanding of the world.

3. What will we explore?

The journey begins with our home, planet Earth. The chapter highlights Earth as the only planet we know that supports life and reminds us that its environment must be protected.

It points towards questions about the variety of life, growth and transformation—for example, a seed growing into a plant or a caterpillar transforming into a butterfly.

Food around us

Food is another area of scientific curiosity. India has a large variety of cuisines and dishes. We can ask what foods are made of and how we can find out.

Materials around us

Everyday objects such as paper, metal, plastic, rubber, magnets, clothes and cups raise questions about materials: What are they made of? Are different objects made from different materials? How can materials be separated?

Water, hot and cold

Water provides more questions. The chapter asks us to notice that water freezes into ice when cooled and boils into steam when heated. Understanding how hot or cold something is can also be important, such as when checking body temperature during a fever.

Beyond Earth

Scientific questions do not stop at Earth. We can also ask about the Sun, the Moon and the millions of stars in the sky.

4. How Science Finds Answers

The chapter uses a simple everyday problem: a pen stops writing. We first ask why. We may guess that the ink has finished, then open the pen and check the refill. If it is empty, the guess is supported. If it is not empty, we make another guess, such as the ink having dried up, and test again.

This is the scientific method in everyday life: a question leads to a possible explanation, which we test through observation or experiment and then examine the result.
Step 1Observe something interesting or something you do not understand.
Step 2Wonder about it and form a question.
Step 3Guess a possible answer and test it through experiments or more observations.
Step 4Analyse the results to see whether they answer the question.

5. Anyone Can Work Like a Scientist

Scientists use the scientific method to solve problems and discover new things. But the chapter points out that anyone following this method is working like a scientist.

  • A cook may wonder why dal has spilled from a cooker and ask whether there was too much water.
  • A bicycle repair person may investigate where air is leaking from a flat tyre.
  • An electrician may check whether a faulty bulb or switch is causing a light not to work.

In this sense, asking questions and finding answers is something we all do in everyday life.

6. Curiosity, Questions and Teamwork

Learning Science develops our ability to find solutions to bigger problems and explore more mysteries of the universe. The chapter says the first and foremost requirement for learning Science well is to be curious and to observe our surroundings keenly.

When we are curious, we start asking how and why. We will not find answers to every question in Grade 6—and that is okay. The journey of Science continues through the next five years and beyond.

Science is also rarely done alone. Scientists around the world often work in large teams. When we cannot find an answer ourselves, we can ask friends to help. Discovering things together can be more fun.

Remember: The world is full of things we do not know—things waiting to be explored. Keep wondering, keep asking questions and keep exploring.

Activities from the Chapter

Activity 1.1 — Let us think and write

Write about a similar problem you tried to solve and describe the steps you took.

Activity 1.2 — Let us think and write

Describe a daily-life situation where you think someone was following the scientific method.

Activity 1.3 — Let us think and write

If you have to ask “Why?” about something, write what you would ask and how you would attempt to find an answer.
