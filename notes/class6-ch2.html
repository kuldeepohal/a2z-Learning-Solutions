2.1 Diversity in Plants and Animals Around Us

Nature contains a large variety of plants and animals. This variety is called biodiversity. During a nature walk, students can observe differences in plant stems, leaves, flowers, animal habitats, food and movement. Each bird may have a distinctive call, which is one example of diversity.

Plants may differ in height, stem hardness, leaf shape and arrangement, flower colour, shape and scent. Animals may differ in where they live, what they eat, how they move, their size, shape and other features. Plants and animals also depend on one anotherâ€”for example, trees provide food and shelter, while animals can help spread seeds.

2.2 Grouping Plants and Animals

Grouping means arranging things into groups based on common features. Grouping makes it easier to understand and study plants and animals using their similarities and differences. Different criteria can be used depending on what we want to study.

Examples of grouping criteria: height, type of stem, presence/absence of flowers, leaf venation, roots, eating habits, habitat, colour and movement.

ðŸƒ Leaf venation

The thin lines seen on leaves are veins. The pattern of veins is called venation.

Reticulate venation: veins form a net-like pattern around a main vein. Example: hibiscus.
Parallel venation: veins run roughly parallel. Examples: banana and grasses.

ðŸŒ¿ Roots

Taproot: one main root with smaller side roots. Example: mustard; hibiscus is another example.
Fibrous roots: a bunch of similar-sized thin roots arise from the base of the stem. Example: common grass.

ðŸŒ¾ Monocots and dicots

Seeds may contain one or two cotyledons. Chickpea has two cotyledons and is a dicot. Maize has one cotyledon and is a monocot.

ðŸ¾ Grouping Animals

Animals can be grouped using features such as movement and body parts used for movement. Ants use legs; goats walk and jump using legs; pigeons and houseflies walk and fly using legs and wings; fish swim using fins. Animals can also be grouped by habitat, food, colour, shape, size and structure.

2.3 Plants and Animals in Different Surroundings

Different regions have different environmental conditions, so their biodiversity varies. Fish have streamlined bodies and fins that help them move through water. Goats use legs to move on land.

Adaptations

Adaptations are special features that enable plants and animals to survive in a particular region. Desert plants such as cactus can have thick, fleshy stems that store water. Deodar trees have a conical shape and sloping branches that help snow slide off. Camels have features that help them survive deserts, including reduced water loss and body features suited to their surroundings.

ðŸ  Habitat

A habitat is the place where a plant or animal lives. It provides food, water, air, shelter and other needs.

Terrestrial habitats: land habitats such as forests, deserts, grasslands and mountains.
Aquatic habitats: water habitats such as ponds, lakes, rivers and oceans.

Animals such as frogs can live in both water and on land; these are called amphibians.

ðŸŒ Biodiversity and conservation

Damage to habitats can remove homes, food and other resources and lead to loss of biodiversity. The chapter highlights conservation efforts including Project Tiger, started in 1973, the Cheetah Reintroduction Project, initiated in 2022, and protected areas for the Great Indian Bustard.

Sacred groves are community-protected patches of forest found across India. They can conserve plants, animals and medicinal plants.
